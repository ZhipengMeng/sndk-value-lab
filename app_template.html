Valuation method and model limits

1. Operating cost bridge

Core sales = FY27 base sales × bit-index × contracted/spot blended ASP − HBF displacement.
COGS = volume-sensitive costs + fixed costs + standalone D&A + management-reference cost wedge.
GM and operating margin are OUTPUTS, not independently chosen.

Contract ASP is clipped between illustrative floor and optional cap. Calibrated extra COGS stays tied to volume, not the pricing of a particular scenario. Future capacity, customer mix, competitor economics and elasticity are assumptions.

2. Fade and terminal value

Fade revenue growth starts from each scenario's structural bit growth × sustainable ASP trend, not the final cyclical growth rate, then converges toward g.
Fade economic profit margin converges toward the TRAILING cycle window ending in the selected explicit year.
FCF = after-tax economic EBIT × (1 − growth / incremental ROIC).
TV = FCFlast fade × (1 + g) / (cost of equity − g).

Two terminal-growth assumptions create the range. The implied PE is a diagnostic. Changes in endpoint can change value if they include an actual trough or recovery; see endpoint comparison. The historical invariant fixture in the tests uses identical cash conversion and sustainable economics at every terminal date.

3. Interim capital

FY2027 remaining cash flow uses a fiscal-year fraction. Explicit-year cash flow is linked to EBIT, incremental direct capex, JV net funding, working capital and economic SBC. Fade-year FCF uses incremental ROIC. Net cash is excluded by default pending reconciliation of securities, deposits, minimum operating cash and obligations.

4. Capital and funding

Both modes check cumulative cash from the valuation date. A gap makes valuation N/M until funding is specified. Distribution mode assumes earlier cash can be retained, so its result is a minimum annual funding need. Buyback mode also subtracts actual modeled repurchases. No external borrowing, automatic share issuance, or intra-year liquidity model is assumed.

Material uncertainty remains. Discount rates, long-run g, incremental ROIC, management-reference costs, actual contract floors, competitors’ expansion, unit economics, HBF commercialization and terminal earnings durability can all change the valuation. The model reports conditional current VALUE, not a trading-floor prediction or an investment recommendation. High-future-margin cases and historical-economics price shocks have not been assigned probabilities.
